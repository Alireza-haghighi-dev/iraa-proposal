## 6. Necessity and significance

The sponsor runs a platform on which organizational, operational and technology needs or challenges are submitted for assessment and for the identification of possible solutions. Its staff review each submission by hand. According to the sponsor's statement of need, reviewing new submissions takes longer as the number of stored records grows beyond the usual volume, and over time this produces a backlog of assessments. The statement gives no figures for review time, backlog or error rates, so the size of the problem is the sponsor's account and has not been measured by this project.

Much of the review effort is search. To assess a new submission, an assessor needs to know whether something similar has already been submitted, whether existing solution examples are known for the need it describes, and whether external patents or solution examples address it. The sponsor's essential requirements follow these questions and add a condition on decisions: the assistant must compare new submissions with those already stored, assess whether relevant existing solution examples can be identified, examine relevant external patents and solution examples, and leave the final approval of every assessment and processing outcome with a person. The intended final product is an assistant that answers the three search questions for each submission, shows the evidence behind each answer, and records the assessor's decision. It does not make assessment decisions.

Commercial innovation-management products cover parts of this. In their own documentation, several vendors describe a duplicate check when an idea is submitted, and some describe scouting for companies, technologies or patents that match a described need (Section 9). In the vendor documentation we read, scouting results are not attached to the assessment record of an individual submission, matching methods are not described, and no accuracy evaluation of the similarity, scoring or scouting functions is reported. The gap is therefore not the absence of similarity search as such. We found no documented and evaluated way to bring matches against stored submissions, solution examples and external prior art together for one submission, under an approval rule that the software itself enforces.

Two findings from the retrieval literature determine how that gap can be closed. On a benchmark of 18 heterogeneous retrieval datasets, BM25 lexical ranking remained a competitive baseline, while re-ranking and late-interaction models achieved the best average zero-shot results at high computational cost (Thakur et al., 2021). On a benchmark covering eight kinds of embedding task, no single text-embedding method dominated across all tasks (Muennighoff et al., 2023). Our searches found no public benchmark of organizational need or challenge submissions. The retrieval method for this assistant therefore has to be chosen by evaluation on text of the kind the sponsor receives, and that evaluation must respect submission order: for duplicate issue reports, the classical evaluation setting was shown to overestimate the performance of retrieval approaches compared with a realistic setting that uses all reports available at the time (Rakha et al., 2018).

Human approval also needs support in the design. A review of studies on automation found that automation bias produces both omission and commission errors when decision aids are imperfect, occurs in expert as well as naive participants, and cannot be prevented by training or instructions (Parasuraman and Manzey, 2010). The assistant is therefore designed to make approval a property of the record that no other path in the software can bypass. It also shows the passage that links each candidate to the submission before any score; this is an untested design choice, and its effect on automation bias is not evaluated in this project.

The sponsor expects faster and more accurate review, better coordination and more consistent organization of submissions. These are expected benefits, not measured outcomes; without a baseline of current review times and error rates the current project cannot test them (Section 12, limits of the method). Section 11 explains why the current phase is funded before the later stages.

## 7. Project objectives

The objective of the current project is to design, build and test a laboratory prototype of an assistant that prepares, for each submission to the sponsor's platform, an assessment record linking three kinds of related material, and that leaves every assessment decision with a human assessor.

The first objective is a similarity function that compares each new submission with the submissions already stored in the platform. For a new submission, the prototype ranks earlier stored submissions and shows the passages or fields that make each one similar. Grouping the whole stored collection into likely duplicates is not part of the current project.

The second objective is a function that checks whether relevant existing solution examples can be identified for a submitted need. Which sources these examples should come from, and whether any of them can be made available to the project, has not been settled with the sponsor. The function is therefore built so that a source can be connected without changing the rest of the prototype, and every example it shows states where it came from. If no source is confirmed during the project, the function is tested on substitute data and the results are reported as substitute-data results.

The third objective is retrieval of external patents, and of external solution examples where a usable source is identified, for each submission. Results are stored in the assessment record with their source and retrieval date. Two separate conditions must both be met before any text derived from a confidential submission is sent to an external service: the sponsor must have authorized that disclosure, and an assessor must have reviewed and approved the exact query text. Approval of a query does not authorize disclosure. Until the sponsor authorizes disclosure, no text derived from a sponsor submission is sent and external retrieval in the project uses public need statements; after authorization, only text within its scope may be sent. Every query, in either case, still requires the assessor's approval.

The fourth objective is a review and approval workflow in which no assessment outcome becomes final without an assessor's approval. The assessor approves the record under their own account; a change after approval creates a new version that must be approved again; approved records are exported as reports. Mandatory labelling of each displayed candidate and generated summaries are not part of the current project.

The fifth objective is an evaluation that states how well the prototype performs and under what conditions. Retrieval quality is measured on a test set ordered by submission date and judged against a written grading guide, by sponsor assessors if they can take part and otherwise by project judges; every result names the group that judged it. Sponsor assessor availability has not been confirmed. The workflow is tested against the approval and disclosure rules. The metrics and targets are listed in Section 8.

At project completion the following will exist: the prototype software with its source code and deployment instructions; a design specification that includes the interface for a later connection to the platform; the evaluation protocol, the judgment and run files, and the substitute test collections whose licences permit handover, identified as substitute data; test and evaluation reports; an information security risk assessment of the prototype's data flows, prepared to support the sponsor's own processes; and a log of assessor decisions in a format that a later stage can use.

The project works under these assumptions. Stored submissions are supplied as a sanitized file export, or a public substitute collection is used and labelled as such. The prototype reads exported data only and does not write to the platform. Submission text is processed within the controlled environment; text derived from a submission goes to an external service only under the two conditions of the third objective. The content of an assessment is configured from information the sponsor provides. Learning from past assessments and integration with the live platform are not objectives of this project; they are described in Section 11.

## 12. Detailed technical implementation method

### Problem-solving logic

The assistant fills one assessment record per submission with the three ranked lists described in Section 8, and only an assessor's approval can make an outcome final. The technical work therefore has two parts with different success conditions. The three retrieval tasks are measured on judged test sets, and their results may turn out well or badly. The approval rule is a property of the record service: it either holds on every path through the software or it does not.

Where a solution-example source can be imported as a collection, finding similar submissions and finding solution examples are the same retrieval problem applied to different collections; a source reachable only through an external service is connected through the connector contract of activity 4 instead. One retrieval service therefore ranks every imported collection, and each collection has its own lexical and vector index. Identical and near-identical submissions are flagged by content hashes and word-shingle fingerprints before ranking. The ranking configuration set is kept deliberately small: BM25 as the lexical baseline, one multilingual dense encoder used without fine-tuning, and the combination of the two by reciprocal rank fusion, which is fixed in advance as the primary configuration. No single method leads across public benchmarks (Thakur et al., 2021; Muennighoff et al., 2023) and no benchmark of need or challenge submissions was found (Section 6), so the evaluation asks one practical question: whether adding semantic retrieval to BM25 helps on this kind of text enough to justify its cost.

### What the project controls and what it does not

The project controls the design, the code, the configuration set, the evaluation protocol, the judging guide, the substitute test collections it builds and the reference environment on which response time is measured. It does not control whether the sponsor releases stored submissions, which solution-example sources count and whether any is available, whether submission text may leave the controlled environment, which patent service grants access, the language of submissions, or whether sponsor assessors can take part in judging. The method has a defined branch for each of these, so that work continues whatever the answer: a substitute schema, the substitute-data track, public need statements for external retrieval, manual entry of assessor-run searches, and project judges (Section 17, risks 1 and 7 to 10; activity 4 for the disclosure rule). Results on substitute data are reported as such and are never presented as performance on sponsor submissions.

### Requirements, constraints and scope

The functions built are those of Section 8; the exclusions are those of Section 7. The sponsor's constraint that the assistant be extensible is met by four defined interfaces: the import format, the retrieval interface, the connector interface and the platform adapter, the last of which is specified but not implemented. The information security constraint is handled through the controls listed in Section 8 and the risk assessment of activity 1. Assessor decisions are logged in a reusable format, and no component learns from them.

### Research sources, tools and test design

Methods are taken from peer-reviewed retrieval and evaluation literature and from official model, library and service documentation; the licences of the candidate models, and their input limits where stated, were read on their official model pages. The project uses no physical modelling or simulation tools. Its models are executable specifications: the record state model is written as a transition table from which the approval tests are generated, and the evaluation protocol is implemented as scripts that recompute every reported figure from stored judgment and run files.

Every retrieval result belongs to one of two data tracks that are never pooled. The sponsor-data track runs only if the sponsor releases data, and is judged by sponsor assessors where they can take part. The substitute-data track uses public or constructed collections and project judges. Test sets are split by submission date. BM25 uses the toolkit's default parameters and reciprocal rank fusion the constant of its original publication, so no ranking parameter is tuned; display thresholds are set on an earlier development segment, and each configuration is run once on the later test segment after the protocol has been frozen. A comparison is reported as better, not worse by more than the margin, inconclusive, worse, or below floor. The prototype is not required to be better than the BM25 baseline.

### 1. Prototype design specification and data-protection baseline

One design specification fixes what activities 3 to 5 implement, so that they are accepted against a written reference. The field map and import-file specification are derived from the sponsor's export schema if one is released and otherwise from a substitute schema. The minimum fields are a platform identifier, title, body text and submission date; items from a solution-example source also carry a source type, availability status and terms note. Every record also carries a disclosure status, which is "confidential" by default for records from a sponsor export and "public" for records from a public collection whose terms allow the use. The assessment template is a configuration file of fields, outcome codes and mandatory items; if the sponsor supplies no assessment content, a placeholder template is used and labelled as such.

The record state model is a transition table with one row for each allowed combination of state (Draft, In review, Submitted, Approved, Returned), event and role, and rejection as the default for every combination not listed. Two properties are stated separately from the table so that they can be checked against it rather than derived from it: no transition into Approved exists except the approval event performed by an authenticated user in the assessor role, and any edit to an approved record creates a new Draft version. If the sponsor requires a second approver, it is added as rows of the table. The interfaces for retrieval, connectors, the record, report export, decision-log export and identity are specified with request and response schemas; the platform adapter is specified only.

The information security risk assessment walks each data flow and trust boundary of the architecture and records, for each, the data that crosses it, the threats of disclosure, unauthorized change, unauthorized state transition, loss of availability and unattributable action, the treatment chosen and the decision left to the sponsor. For the external-query flow it records both controls: the disclosure clearance of the text and the assessor's approval of the exact query. The same task produces the permission matrix for the three project roles (assessor, administrator, evaluator) and a retention and deletion procedure. By default, imported sponsor data, test collections derived from it and cached external results are deleted at project close unless the sponsor decides otherwise; substitute collections built from public sources, judgment files and run files are retained and handed over where their licences permit. The reference environment is specified by hardware class, operating system and runtime versions, and the components in the tool table are pinned to exact versions, with each licence file read on the pinned version.

In the first month a small end-to-end prototype (import of a substitute file, BM25 search, display of the results) checks the chosen libraries and the environment before the full build. The activity is accepted at a design review against a checklist: every function in scope maps to a component, an interface and a test; the transition table enumerates every state, event and role used by the approval tests; and every open owner input is listed with the default applied. If sponsor inputs on the export format, assessment content, approval model and accounts do not arrive, the substitute schema and defaults keep the build moving, and a later change is a configuration change, not a redesign (Section 17). The specification also fixes the funded function set, so that an optional function can enter the build only through a recorded owner decision.

### 2. Evaluation protocol, test collections and evaluation harness

The evaluation tooling is built and checked before any test pool is judged, because a protocol changed after test data have been seen cannot support a comparison. The activity starts by checking the licence of each substitute candidate on its original distribution. For CQADupStack, a duplicate-question collection built from Stack Exchange forums (Hoogeveen et al., 2015), no data licence could be established: the original distribution page states none, the upstream content was licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 at the time of the 2014 data dump, and third-party copies carry conflicting statements. The collection may not be used until four conditions are recorded: a data licence from the original distribution, compatibility with the planned evaluation use, consistency with the upstream licence, and an owner decision. If these conditions are not met, another candidate that passes the same check is used, and the harness is checked against another benchmark with published reference runs and an acceptable licence.

The protocol and judging guide fix the grade scales with worked examples; the three configurations; the metrics, namely nDCG@10 (Järvelin and Kekäläinen, 2002) for similar submissions, P@5 at grade 2 or above for solution examples and success@10 for external prior art, with Recall@20, MRR, bpref and judged@10 as secondary and completeness measures; the planning values and their confirmation status; the decision rule; and the acceptance targets. The protocol records its freeze date. Each requirement has one pre-specified primary comparison: the hybrid configuration against BM25 for similar submissions and, on each registered source, for solution examples; edited automatic queries against unaided keyword queries for external prior art. The result is the mean paired difference in the primary metric with a two-sided 95% paired bootstrap interval, and a paired randomization test p-value is reported beside it. For the two ranking tasks the margin is 0.05, a planning value: the hybrid configuration is described as not worse than BM25 by more than the margin only if the lower bound of the interval lies above minus the margin, as better only if the lower bound lies above zero, as worse only if the upper bound lies below minus the margin, and otherwise as inconclusive. Reading the interval first against the margin and then against zero follows the closed-testing argument of the CPMP points to consider on switching between superiority and non-inferiority (CPMP, 2000). The EMA page for that document states that it is superseded by the guideline on non-inferiority and equivalence comparisons in clinical trials, but that guideline (EMA/301654/2025) was published as a draft for consultation until 31 May 2026 and its page, checked on 21 November 2026, gives no adopted version or legal effective date; the interval criterion is checked against the adopted text, if any, before the protocol is frozen. The external prior-art comparison is reported as an estimate with its interval and no inferential claim. Each primary comparison answers a different requirement and is reported on its own; dense-only results and secondary metrics are descriptive. Absence of relevant items is stated only at the level checked: the judged pool or a deeper sampled check. Bpref is reported because standard measures become unreliable when relevance judgments are substantially incomplete (Buckley and Voorhees, 2004).

Test collections are built per track with time-ordered development and test splits. Each collection carries its source, licence note, track label, split dates and checksum, and a manifest script rejects any collection that mixes tracks. If no solution-example source is confirmed and the owner approves it, a substitute is constructed for solution-example retrieval: project judges write 50 need statements in the form of a submission, which are searched against a public solution-description source whose terms pass the licence check. Thirty of the same statements serve as the public need statements for external prior-art evaluation, so one set is written for both tasks. Without the substitute, the solution-example target could not be tested on any data.

The harness reads run files in the standard six-column format, computes the metrics through the toolkit's evaluation functions and through project code for success@10 and judged@10, and implements, with established statistical libraries where they provide the procedure, the paired bootstrap, the sign-flip randomization test, Clopper–Pearson intervals for single proportions such as the "no example found" rate (Clopper and Pearson, 1934) and, for the paired binary outcome of external prior art, a score-type interval for paired proportions fixed in the protocol. The randomization and bootstrap tests are used because, in a comparison on TREC ad-hoc runs, they agreed closely with the t-test while the Wilcoxon and sign tests were less able to detect significance and could produce false detections (Smucker et al., 2007). Metric code is unit-tested against hand-computed examples. A judging screen shows one query and one item at a time, in random order, without the name of the configuration that retrieved the item, and records the grade and the time taken. The harness is accepted when it reproduces a published BM25 reference run within rounding; a harness that cannot reproduce a published figure is not used for any comparison. The frozen protocol, the time-ordered split and the single test run keep tuning out of the test results; pooling all three configurations limits the bias against a configuration that retrieves items the others miss.

### 3. Ingestion and retrieval for similar submissions and solution examples

The ingestion adapter parses delimited or JSON Lines files against the field map and writes an import report listing accepted rows, rejected rows with the reason, and exact duplicates found by content hash. Each solution-example source is entered in a source registry with its source type, availability status and terms note, and every stored item inherits these fields so that they can be shown beside each result. The normalization pipeline cleans text, computes exact-copy hashes and word-shingle fingerprints for the near-duplicate baseline (Broder, 1997), stores a language tag, and splits long texts into overlapping passages sized to each encoder's input limit (512 tokens for multilingual-e5-base). It counts the passages each record produces, so that truncation is visible for each collection.

For each registered collection the index layer builds one lexical index over passages that carry their record's title and one vector index over L2-normalized passage embeddings from the dense encoder, and stores the index version with every query record. The retrieval service runs three configurations behind one interface: BM25 (Robertson and Zaragoza, 2009); the dense encoder without fine-tuning, since no labelled training pairs exist; and reciprocal rank fusion of the two (Cormack et al., 2009). The fingerprint baseline flags exact and near-identical submissions alongside the ranked list. A record's score is its highest passage score, and that passage is returned as the evidence shown before the score. A similar-submission query searches only items dated before the submission. For vector search this is done by filtered search if the pinned Faiss version supports it, and otherwise by retrieving extra candidates and filtering them, with the number of candidates lost to filtering logged. The prototype and the evaluation use exact vector search; at the corpus sizes tested, approximate search is not needed.

The display threshold for similar submissions and the "no example above threshold" signal for solution examples are set on the development split only, by a rule fixed in the protocol, and are not changed after the test run. Encoding and search times are measured on the reference environment at 10,000 and 100,000 items, the planning sizes; a configuration that cannot meet the response-time target of 5 seconds at the 95th percentile, a planning value, is reported as such and is not used as the default. Larger collections are outside the tested range and would need approximate search or index compression. Unit and integration tests cover parsing, validation, hashing, segmentation, index building, date filtering and the presence of an evidence passage for every candidate. If the sponsor releases an export, it is parsed against the field map as a feasibility test.

The activity is accepted when each of the three configurations produces a run file on the development split, every candidate carries an evidence passage, and the import report lists rejected rows with reasons. Language, input-length and hardware risks and their mitigations are listed in Section 17 (risks 2 to 5).

### 4. External prior-art retrieval with query approval

The connector framework defines a plug-in contract for any external source: a query with an optional maximum number of results returns items with source name, external identifier, title, abstract text, date, link, terms note and retrieval time. The framework applies a configurable rate limiter set below the provider's published thresholds, which for EPO Open Patent Services are guidance values, not fixed limits: about 1 Mbit per second and 4 GB per week free under the fair-use charter, with a yearly subscription above that, and, according to the OPS FAQ, one request per second for family-related actions and ten searches per minute per IP address as a rule, higher or lower depending on operational circumstances. It retries with back-off when requests are refused and caches every result in the content store with its provenance, so that a record can be reproduced if the external source changes. One connector is built for the service the owner selects. EPO Open Patent Services is recommended because its registration route, free tier and terms permitting inclusion of its data in the user's own products are documented (European Patent Office, OPS documentation); key issuance for the PatentsView API is uncertain, and the Lens terms of use have not been read. A smoke test with public test queries checks authentication, parsing, provenance and behaviour under rate limiting.

A candidate query is built from the submission's title and its highest-weighted passages by selecting terms with TF-IDF weights computed against the stored collection, and the connector translates it into the service's query syntax. Text leaves the controlled environment only when two separate conditions are both met. The first is disclosure clearance: the record's disclosure status must be "public", or a sponsor authorization recorded by the administrator must cover the record and field the text was derived from. The second is query approval: the assessor sees the exact text that would be sent, may shorten or redact it, and approves it. Query approval never substitutes for disclosure clearance. For a sponsor submission without clearance, the assessment record states that external retrieval was not run. The disclosure clearance, the approval, the approver and the exact text sent are written to the audit log.

Both conditions are enforced at two levels. The record service refuses to pass text to the connector unless the clearance and the approval are both present, and the reference environment allows outbound connections only from the connector process to the configured service hosts, so that a bypass attempt from another component fails at the network level. The connector computes a hash of the request payload it sends and logs it beside the hash of the approved text; the disclosure test compares the two for every query and also attempts to send confidential text with an approved query but no authorization, which must be refused.

If no patent service grants access, the assessor runs the search in the service's own interface and enters the results through a manual-entry form with the same provenance fields. Before the form accepts results, the assessor records in the prototype the exact query text used and the disclosure clearance it relies on; the route applies only to cleared text, the prototype cannot technically prevent text being typed outside it, and manual queries are reported separately. The external prior-art evaluation is then marked as not run against a connected service. Tests cover the connector contract, attempted bypasses of either condition through the interface, the service layer and direct network calls, and the completeness of provenance on every stored result. A second patent connector is built only if the owner selects more than one service, with the same contract and tests. The evaluation compares two formulations for each need statement: an unaided keyword query written by a judge who has not seen the automatic query, and the automatic query after a different judge, acting in the assessor role, has edited it at the approval step. Because this evaluation uses public need statements, it does not depend on the disclosure decision.

### 5. Assessment record, review interface and approval controls

The record service is the only component that changes record state. It loads the transition table from the design specification at start-up and rejects any request whose combination of state, event and role is not in the table. The outcome field is writable only inside the approval transaction, and a database trigger rejects any other write to it, so that the rule holds even for code paths that bypass the service layer. Approval binds the record version to the authenticated assessor, and every approved record stores the versions of the models, indexes, connector and template used. An edit to an approved record creates a new Draft version that must be approved again.

Access control uses project-managed accounts through the web framework's authentication, with salted password hashing and expiring sessions, and checks permissions on every call to the record interface. The audit log is an insert-only table: the application's database role has no update or delete permission on it, and each entry stores a hash of the previous entry, so that removal or alteration of an entry is detected by recomputing the chain. The review screens show, for each listed item in the three result lists, the matching passage first, then the source type, availability status and terms note, and only then the score. The query-approval screen, the assessment template form and the approve and return actions are shown only to the assessor role, and scripted browser tests check both the display order and the role restriction. Approved records are exported as a human-readable report and as a machine-readable file validated against the schema in the design specification. The decision log is exported with submission identifier, candidate identifier, collection, label, reason, assessor role and time, without names, and no component retrains on it.

The approval-enforcement test suite is generated from the transition table. It attempts every combination of state, event and role, including the evaluator and administrator roles and unauthenticated requests, through the interface and directly against the service layer; and adds expired sessions and replayed requests. The test oracle is the pair of properties stated in activity 1, checked on the database after every step, not the transition table itself, so that an error in the table is caught rather than reproduced. The re-approval test changes each editable field of an approved record once and checks that a new version requiring approval is created; it also recomputes the audit-log hash chain. The pass criterion is zero records reaching Approved without an approval event and zero changes escaping re-approval, and coverage is reported against the transition table. The approval model is not yet settled with the sponsor: if a second approver or other approving roles are required, the transition table changes and the generated suite follows it without new test code.

### 6. Prototype integration and evaluation

The outputs of activities 3 to 5 are integrated into one release, built from tagged sources by deployment scripts on a clean instance of the reference environment; the release is accepted when all their test suites pass on it. Pilot judging follows on the ten development queries per task. It measures agreement between judges, using Krippendorff's alpha on the ordinal scale (Hayes and Krippendorff, 2007) and Cohen's kappa on the binary split (Cohen, 1960), and the time per judgment. It allows one revision of the judging guide, and the graded scales for similar submissions and solution examples are collapsed to three levels if alpha stays below 0.67 after the revision; 0.67 is a planning value set by the project. The planned test sets are 50 queries for similar submissions, 40 need statements per solution-example source and 30 need statements for external prior art, sized for the judging time of a six-month project, not for a target statistical power. Error rates in retrieval comparisons depend on the number of topics and on the size of the observed difference (Voorhees and Buckley, 2002); the pilot standard deviation of per-query differences therefore gives the expected width of each interval at the planned size, and the protocol records before the test run whether a statement against the margin is likely to be reachable. If it is not, the report says so in advance and presents the comparison as an estimate. Absolute floors are fixed and dated before the test run.

The primary configuration is fixed in the protocol, so development data are used only for thresholds and the pilot. Test pools are judged blind to configuration, with 20% of query-item pairs judged by two judges and disagreements adjudicated by a third. For five randomly sampled test queries per task and track whose judged pool contains no relevant item, items ranked 11 to 30 by any configuration are also judged. The test split is run once per configuration, and the approval, re-approval, disclosure, attribution and response-time tests are run on the release.

The evaluation report states every result with its data track, confidence interval and outcome label, never combines sponsor-data and substitute-data results, and reports a secondary comparison whose interval includes zero as "no supported difference". It reports the "no example found" error rate as a lower bound measured against the judged pool, beside the estimate from the deeper check. If on a track the hybrid configuration is not shown to be not worse than BM25 by more than the margin, or falls below its floor, the report says so and the as-built specification makes BM25 the default configuration for that kind of collection. Because relevance judgments vary between judges (Voorhees, 2000), the comparisons are repeated with each judge's own grades on the double-judged subset to check whether the direction of each result is stable. An error analysis classifies the hybrid configuration's failures on the test queries, for example vocabulary mismatch, long or multi-topic text and language, and directs corrective development before handover; corrected code is described in the report and is not re-scored on the test split. Judging and sample-size risks are listed in Section 17 (risks 6 and 12). If sponsor assessors can take part, a structured walk-through of the review screens records how they use them; the evaluation does not depend on it.

### 7. Technical documentation and handover

The design specification is updated to the built prototype and accompanied by deployment and operation instructions for the reference environment. The instructions are accepted when a team member who did not write the deployment scripts deploys the release from them on a clean instance and the rehearsal is recorded. A final reproducibility check recomputes every reported figure from the stored run and judgment files. The information security risk assessment is updated to the built system, and imported sponsor data, test collections derived from it and cached external results are deleted as the retention procedure specifies, with a deletion record. The source code and configuration are packaged with an inventory listing every third-party component and model with its version, licence and training-data terms, and a scan confirms that no sponsor data is in the package. A handover demonstration on the release shows the three result lists and the approval and re-approval workflow, and acceptance or listed reservations are recorded. Continuity notes for later stages describe the decision-log format, the platform adapter specification and the limits found in evaluation.

### Limits of the method

The evaluation measures retrieval quality on judged test sets and the conformance of the workflow to the approval and disclosure rules. It does not measure assessment speed, assessor workload or decision accuracy in live use, because no baseline review time or error rate exists; the benefits expected in the statement of need remain hypotheses for a later timed study. The test sets are small, so several comparisons may end inconclusive, and the report says so rather than claiming equivalence. Results on substitute data describe the methods on that data and do not predict results on sponsor submissions.
